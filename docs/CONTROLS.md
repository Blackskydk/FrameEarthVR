# Controls

All bindings live in `Assets/EarthVR/Input/Resources/EarthVRInputActions.inputactions`. Gameplay code consumes `IEarthVRInput`, not device buttons. Rebind there without changing navigation code.

## Controller defaults

| Hand/control | Logical action | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant/right thumbstick | Scale-and-fly | Flight uses adaptive speed; Grounded mode keeps feet on the surface and converts vertical intent into gradual scale changes |
| Right trigger only | Grab | Point at visible terrain and hold to grab that exact point. The selected geographic point is hard-pinned to the pointer endpoint at its original depth: translating or rotating the hand moves the world immediately, with no gain, spring, or pointer-first dead travel. The left trigger never grabs. Releasing while moving briefly carries the world's momentum, decaying to a stop. The ray must hit an actual physics surface — aiming at open sky, an unloaded gap, or past the horizon simply does not grab anything |
| Either grip | Rotate | Hold and turn the controller to yaw Earth around you; pitch and roll are ignored so the ground stays underneath. In the tilted planetary view, the same gesture freely rotates Earth around its center |
| Right shoulder (Steam Frame) | Speed boost | Hold in Flight or Grounded mode for 8× travel speed; it exclusively owns locomotion while held, so it never also grabs, rotates, or scales Earth |
| Left View / pause button (Steam Frame) | OpenMenu | Show/hide the left-hand menu and destination globe together. Both start hidden and close automatically when travel begins |
| Left D-pad Up (Steam Frame) / left primary button (A / X) on other controllers | ToggleGlobeOverview | Tilt the ground into a north-up map without changing zoom; right-trigger-drag or either grip rotates Earth, right-stick forward/back zooms toward the pointed location, a trigger click travels, and the same overview button returns |
| Left D-pad Right (Steam Frame) | ToggleMovementMode | Toggle directly between Flight and Grounded mode |
| Left D-pad Down (Steam Frame) / left secondary button (B / Y) on other controllers | ResetView | Restore a comfortable upright orientation |
| Point at Sun/Moon + trigger | Time of day | Drag along the physically valid solar path for the current date and map location; the interaction owns the trigger until release |

## Car mode

Choose `ENTER CAR MODE` from the hand menu to place a stable cockpit around the viewer and settle the vehicle onto the streamed surface. The right stick becomes car controls: forward accelerates, back brakes and reverses, and left/right steers. Hold the right shoulder for a modest turbo increase. The car follows terrain and photogrammetry height through the normal grounding system, while steep terrain and building walls stop forward motion. World grabbing, world rotation, and grounded scaling are disabled while driving so they cannot fight the vehicle. Open the menu and choose `EXIT CAR MODE` to return to the locomotion mode used before entering; Left D-pad Right also exits to Flight.

Keyboard test fallbacks: `W`/`S` fly forward/reverse, `R` resets upright, `G` toggles movement mode, and `O` toggles planetary overview. These are diagnostic conveniences for a non-VR Play Mode smoke test, not the primary locomotion scheme.

In Flight mode, aimed thumbstick movement is normal three-dimensional flight with altitude-adaptive speed. In Grounded mode, aim the right controller nearly straight up or down (within about 20 degrees of vertical) and push its stick forward/back to resize. Walking stops during this gesture. A single geographic foot anchor is retained for the entire gesture, and terrain-height corrections pause until the stick is released. Full input scales at 1.6 doublings per second; partial stick input scales more slowly. At ordinary controller angles the stick walks, without resizing. Controller roll and sideways stick input do not cause resizing.

New sessions choose a different destination from a curated mix of national parks and cities, including Zion, Yosemite, Banff, Tokyo, Cape Town, and Sydney; the immediately previous launch location is skipped. Sessions start at local solar noon for the selected location, ensuring the map initially loads in daylight. Every completed journey also selects local solar noon at the destination. If the destination is in polar night on the current simulation date, the date moves to that hemisphere's summer solstice so the landing is still sunlit. A white guide draws only the above-horizon arc: the Sun's arc by day and the Moon handle's antipodal arc by night. It is hidden until the user looks toward the active body, starts appearing within 10 degrees, and reaches full visibility within 3 degrees. The arc is brightest beside the active body and falls off with path distance. The Sun or Moon grows when either controller is accurately aimed at its enlarged invisible hit area, providing grab-ready feedback. Trigger-dragging the body chooses time along its calculated arc and captures the trigger until release. At night the larger, crater-shaded Moon makes returning to daylight straightforward.

Directional sunlight, cool moon fill, soft shadows, ambient/reflection intensity, warm sunset grading, exposure, procedural sky gradients, stars, and a subtle Milky Way respond to solar elevation. While planetary overview is active, astronomical time is preserved but sunlight temporarily follows the viewer-to-Earth axis, keeping the entire visible hemisphere illuminated while Earth rotates. Normal time-of-day lighting resumes on exit. The sky shader uses Unity's stereo instancing macros, and the Sun/Moon handles sit 500 metres away to minimize binocular disparity.

The right-hand pointer is low-opacity white, retains a visible angular width over long distances, stops at the first physics surface it hits, and shows a translucent white hit marker on that surface. The left hand has no beam because it carries the destination globe. Google photogrammetry physics meshes must remain enabled for exact point grabbing. Earth drag stores exactly one selected point in ECEF coordinates for the entire right-trigger hold. Navigation Space is solved absolutely from the latest tracked controller pose and the same constraint is solved again after OpenXR's final before-render pose update, preventing a visible one-pose lag. Flight fixes the original pointer depth. Grounded fixes the tracking floor at its grab-start height and solves pointer depth so the same selected ECEF point remains exactly on the live ray without lifting the user; independent ground correction yields during the hold so it cannot fight that joint constraint.

Flight→Grounded switching changes apparent world scale while leaving the eyes (camera) fixed: Grounded solves for the one scale at which the ground directly below lands exactly at the current tracking-floor height — i.e. feet-on-the-ground, without moving the observer to get there — directly against Cesium's real scale-to-world mapping. Switching back to Flight changes only the locomotion constraint and preserves that user scale, so the curved-Earth view does not expand back into an apparently flat plane or expose a distant world edge. Finding "the ground directly below" tries an instant physics raycast first, then falls back to Cesium's own unbounded height query (a short asynchronous wait, since Flight altitude can be far beyond where any raycast usefully reaches or where terrain colliders are even loaded); the eye/floor reference is captured before that wait starts, so nothing about the viewpoint can drift while the query is in flight, and a second mode switch discards a stale result. The Grounded perspective shift plays out smoothly over about three quarters of a second in log scale. Ground-height following probes below the tracking floor rather than the camera, so physically stepping around a room-scale play area never causes an automatic climb onto a nearby roof or wall. It also samples ahead along the current heading and eases quickly upward rather than snapping across a skyline's rooftop/gap/rooftop pattern.

The Grounded transition first performs a visually equivalent ECEF rebase at the viewer, then freezes locomotion while it animates only Cesium scale. The exact two-sample solve is linear in `1/scale`. A terrain-query timeout falls back to the WGS84 surface instead of leaving Grounded without a floor, and Grounded floor correction remains active while stationary as well as while travelling. Flight keeps the scale at which it was entered; search destinations still deliberately begin at the normal 1× default.

Flight mode's ground-approach speed safety samples straight down and a short distance ahead along the current heading, and tightens the speed cap instantly but relaxes it back out gradually — this keeps a low pass over a dense city skyline (rooftop, gap, rooftop, gap) from reading as jumpy or stuttery the way reacting instantly to every gap between buildings did. A swept camera-volume collision test additionally clamps each Flight displacement against loaded terrain, including during boost, so high frame-to-frame travel cannot tunnel through a surface merely because the speed safety did not stop completely.

Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles use a fixed 6 px PC / 10 px Frame screen-space error target, sibling preloading, 20 concurrent loads, larger caches, and no Cesium horizon-fog culling. The camera far plane always clears the mathematically computed spherical-Earth horizon, while a sky-colored linear haze begins near that horizon above 10 km. Together, the curved Cesium surface remains visible much farther out and an unavoidable streaming boundary dissolves into atmosphere instead of reading as a hard tile edge.

The rounded hand interface follows the miniature Earth, with Flight/Grounded, comfort-vignette, quick-favorite, and menu controls on its right. The main panel also contains the Car mode entry/exit control. The toggles state their current value and change color with it. The left View/menu button shows or hides the complete interface, which always reopens on its main page. Looking toward or away from it does not change visibility. The globe's carry offset and the upright panels do not inherit controller or head roll. Starting any travel closes it, and it cannot be reopened until the journey ends. Point the dominant-controller ray at a control and press trigger; every button's physical hit area is intentionally a little larger than its drawn size for easier aiming, capped short of overlapping its neighbors. Mode presses are ignored while a Flight/Grounded/Car transition is still settling. There is no thumbstick turning outside Car mode.

`FAVORITES & RECENT PLACES` opens the Favorites panel. `FAVORITE THIS VIEW` or the round star beside the globe saves the complete viewpoint: coordinates, altitude, heading, scale, movement mode, and solar time. Favorites persist as local JSON; recent places are automatically added by search, favorites/recents travel, and globe picks, deduplicated, and capped at twelve. Selecting any saved row uses a comfort fade, normalizes its lighting to destination daylight, and keeps the view black until Cesium reports at least 85% of the destination view loaded for a stable moment. A 20-second timeout guarantees recovery when the network or tile service fails.

The NASA Blue Marble destination globe appears just above the left controller while the hand menu is open, independent of user scale. A green “You are here” marker continuously tracks the live longitude/latitude. The globe follows left-hand position but maintains its own stable orientation instead of rotating with the hand. Pointing at it smoothly enlarges it by 35%. Hold the right trigger and sweep the pointer across its surface to rotate it like a trackball; a short click without dragging marks a location, and a second click near the same point within four seconds travels there. Its corrected texture and hit-coordinate conventions share the same longitude/latitude projection, so the visible map aligns with the selected coordinate. The first click prevents accidental intercontinental jumps, and an interaction that starts on the globe remains owned by the globe until trigger release rather than falling through into terrain grabbing.

The menu's `SEARCH FOR A LOCATION` button opens the search page on the same hand panel. As text is entered, up to three local suggestions appear from the bundled cities, geographic landmarks, national parks and other well-known POIs, plus personal bookmarks and recent places. Choosing a suggestion travels immediately without a network request; pressing `SEARCH` still queries Nominatim for addresses and anything outside the bundled catalog.

Left D-pad Up on the Steam Frame (the left primary button on other controllers) tilts the streamed Cesium ground smoothly into a north-up map in front of the viewer. Entry changes orientation only: it does not change altitude, scale, or zoom. Hold the right trigger on a surface location and sweep the pointer to spin Earth like a physical trackball: the originally touched location stays attached to the pointer, Earth's center remains fixed in front of the viewer, and a virtual limb projection gives a natural stop after the ray leaves the visible silhouette. Either grip also provides free three-axis Earth rotation in this view. Release after a trigger drag without travelling. Pull the right stick back to zoom out and push it forward to zoom in. On inward zoom, one ECEF surface point is captured for the whole stick gesture and remains pinned to the live pointer ray rather than being resampled or approximated with longitude/latitude interpolation. The local Cesium frame is rebased at the current view center and all cached orientation state is recalculated, keeping the opposite hemisphere and the ±180° seam numerically stable without introducing an unsolicited jump or roll. A short trigger click travels to the pointed location. Major cities and recognizable geographic places fade in earlier than before, use larger bold type with a dark contrast shadow, and remain culled for the far hemisphere and overlapping names. These labels come from a small catalog bundled with the app and make no network or billable places/geocoding API request. Destination travel follows a great-circle orbit at the zoom level you chose, then descends continuously while progressively finer Cesium tiles stream. Descent speed automatically eases when tile loading falls behind; it never fades to black or teleports at the end. Selection uses the WGS84 ellipsoid when no terrain collider is present, so oceans and temporarily unloaded tiles remain valid destinations. Pressing the overview button again follows the seamless path back to the saved viewpoint. The map view renders only the Cesium surface—there is no overlapping decorative globe—and Cesium retains parent tiles until replacement children are ready to prevent cracks during refinement.

During continuous travel, the runtime automatically rebases the Cesium origin whenever the camera becomes 2,000 Unity metres from it. The camera's ECEF position and orientation are preserved, so this has no control of its own and should be visually invisible.

Search uses EarthVR's built-in world-space keyboard. `EarthVRWristMenu.Initialize` accepts optional keyboard integrations through the `ISystemKeyboardProvider` extension point, but none is enabled in the supported project configuration.

A peripheral comfort vignette is available but off by default. When enabled from the hand menu, it darkens gradually toward the edges of view as the camera's actual physical speed through space increases (not raw geographic speed, which says nothing about how fast the view visually moves once the world is scaled), and fades back out once movement slows or stops.

In Flight mode, forward speed eases down automatically as the ground below gets close, so a fast, low pass over terrain settles instead of flying straight into a hillside — unless the shoulder boost is held, which is treated as a deliberate request for maximum speed and bypasses this safety net entirely. Away from the ground, cruise speed grows to at least 35% of the current altitude per second (still capped by the configured maximum), making regional and intercontinental travel progressively faster as more of Earth becomes visible. The same eased 8× shoulder multiplier applies to Grounded travel. A procedural, non-spatial soaring-wind bed follows actual Flight speed and stick intent, then fades smoothly when movement stops or Grounded mode is selected.

Grounded mode keeps at least 1.5 cm of physical clearance above the currently loaded collision surface. If thumbstick travel or a newly refined photogrammetry tile places terrain above the tracking floor, the floor eases up to meet it; corrections toward lower ground use a longer smoothing time. Ground correction pauses during world grabbing and manual resizing so it cannot move their geographic anchors.
